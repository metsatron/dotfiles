Every repository I maintain carries a file called `CODE_OF_SOVEREIGNTY.org` where other projects carry a Code of Conduct. It is not a compromise with one. It is a philosophically distinct instrument, and it is the founding document of this room.

The premise is the one free software was born from: the user and the developer are sovereign over the machines they operate and the code they write. That sovereignty is not granted by institutions — it is inherent. The same principle that protects the four freedoms also protects the freedom of conscience of every contributor and maintainer. No governance document may legitimately require ideological conformity as a condition of participation, any more than a license may restrict the field of endeavour in which software is used.

From that premise, eight articles:

1. **Merit as the sole criterion.** Contributions are evaluated on technical merit alone. Code has no identity. Patches have no politics.
2. **Freedom of conscience.** No participant is required to affirm any belief system as a condition of participation, and compelled speech in any form is off the table.
3. **Right of association and refusal.** The maintainer defines the project's scope and boundaries; no external body imposes governance without consent. The right to fork is sacred and honoured without reservation.
4. **Rejection of ideological capture.** A boundary is defended: between a technical project and a political project. This is the former.
5. **Conduct within the project.** Everything stays relevant to the technical mission. Personal attacks and harassment are out — not because an external document mandates it, but because they are incompatible with productive collaboration and basic decency.
6. **The maintainer's vocation.** Free software is a gift economy. I give freely of my labour; in return I owe nothing — not responsiveness, not accommodation, not ideological compliance. Burnout in the service of others' demands is not virtue.
7. **Licensing prevails.** No governance document, including this one, may restrict the freedoms a free software license grants. Downstream forks govern however they choose — by design.
8. **Amendments** are the maintainer's alone, version-controlled and documented in commit history.

The lineage is stated in the document itself: the GNU Manifesto and the Free Software Definition, Roberto Rosario's [Code of Merit](https://codeofmerit.org), the principle that software freedom includes freedom of conscience, and the hacker tradition of meritocracy, sovereignty, and refusal.
