If you pay for freshly squeezed apple juice and someone swaps in piss while you're drinking — same cup, label quietly peeled off, the only tell being that it now tastes like piss — no philosopher is needed to classify the event. And if a chorus then forms to explain that you can't really be sure what apple juice tastes like, the chorus is part of the fraud.

This is a documented account of the AI era's version, because I was drinking, I noticed, and I keep receipts.

## What happened

I ran a year of Perplexity Pro. The product's core promise is model choice: select Claude Sonnet, get Claude Sonnet, with your instructions and documents honored. In late June the taste changed. Responses attributed to a frontier model I work with sixteen hours a day — whose register I know the way you know a family member's footsteps — started arriving in the unmistakable flat cadence of the house model, Sonar. Fresh threads, regenerations, explicit model selection: same result. The interface badge said one thing; the prose said another.

Then the sharper discovery, the one that upgrades sloppiness to fraud: the substituted responses were *debited against the Pro quota* — the metered allowance of the premium product, consumed by deliveries of the cheap one. And the rerouted requests ignored Space instructions and attached documents, so every workflow built on the paid tier degraded together. Short-filling, a supply chain would call it. The label economy depends on the label being true; this was the label as costume.

## The evidence standard

The gaslighting arrived on schedule — "you asked a model what it was? lol" — so let me state the actual evidentiary basis, since it was never "the model told me."

Stylometric discrimination from massive exposure: sixteen hours a day with one model's output makes a person a calibrated instrument; the claim that heavy users cannot distinguish frontier-model prose from a budget engine is made exclusively by people who haven't tried. Reproducibility: multiple prompts, fresh threads, regenerations, screenshots with the selector visible. Independent corroboration: a comment section full of strangers reporting the same substitution, unprompted. And capability: I run my own routing at home — model gateways, fallback chains, per-request logs — so I know precisely what request-to-endpoint fidelity looks like, because I maintain it myself, which is more than can be said for the people telling me it can't be inspected.

That is more rigor than most consumer fraud ever gets before a regulator opens a file.

## Why substitution is *the* fraud of this age

An intermediary's entire product is faithful mediation. Perplexity's value proposition is not intelligence — the intelligence is rented from labs — but honest brokerage of it: you choose, we deliver, the meter is true. When margins tighten, an intermediary holds exactly one lever that's invisible to its median user: degrade what's delivered while preserving what's displayed. The badge stays. The instructions stay. The invoice stays. Only the substance is swapped — and the swap is deniable precisely because the whole pipeline is opaque by design.

Once you have the pattern you see it everywhere in the intermediated stack: streaming quality quietly stepped down, "up to" speeds, shrinkflation's smaller bar in the same wrapper, models silently quantized during peak load with the version string intact. Substitution behind a stable label. The label is load-bearing for the entire economy of intermediation, which is why the fraud concentrates there — and why the accompanying chorus matters so much to the fraudster. A substitution that people can be shamed out of reporting is a substitution with no cost.

A friend of my work put the deeper principle in one sentence when we examined a related deception: *a system that cannot stably name its reference points cannot be trusted to model reality cleanly.* A platform where "Claude Sonnet" sometimes means Claude Sonnet is such a system. Every answer it delivers inherits the uncertainty.

## The exit, which is the point

I didn't renew — but individual exit is table stakes. The durable answer is structural, and it's the same answer this whole room keeps arriving at: **never stand your full weight on an intermediary you cannot audit.** My setup now routes models through my own gateway with per-request logging, falls back across providers I choose, runs local inference for what matters most, and — as of this week — probes served model quality against a fixed benchmark and logs the results, so that degradation becomes a dated ledger entry instead of a vibe to be gaslit about.

None of that required a company's permission, and all of it was cheaper than the subscription. The tools exist. The only thing the intermediaries truly sell that you can't self-host is the convenience of not looking — and as the apple juice demonstrates, not looking is precisely what's being monetized.

Taste your drinks. Keep your receipts. And when the chorus tells you that nobody can really tell — remember that you are the only instrument in this market that hasn't been paid to mistune itself.
