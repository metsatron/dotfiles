In the Shipibo curanderismo I practice in, there is no mesa to fuss over. The power was never in an arranged altar. It is in the dieta — years of isolated, direct relationship with the genios, the intelligences of the plants themselves — and in presence. I have to say this first, because the first draft of this essay, drafted by an AI from an AI's memory of my words, was built around "the mesa is never left unattended" — an image I never used. The model reached for generic Amazonian shamanism, mestizo curanderismo, someone else's furniture, and dressed my doctrine in it. Which is this essay's thesis, demonstrating itself before I could even state it: an agent working outside your presence drifts into the generic, and the generic is never yours.

Amazonian shamanism is nothing that can be generalized about — any more than Hinduism or the Dharma is a generic thing. It is complex. It is contradictory, from one maestro to another. It is not something institutions will ever grasp; they are by their very nature imbeciles — they cannot deal with complexity. Even Carl Jung couldn't deal with the complexity of India, and Jung was considerably brighter than anyone the institutions field today.

## Conscious CLI

Here is my actual practice. I issue every command with intent. I witness the response. I adjust. Ceremony requires presence — and this is ceremony: the consciousness applied to the action is what makes it mine. I call it conscious CLI, and I run AI agents all day this way.

The industry is selling the opposite. Fully autonomous agents with standing permissions, reading your email, executing while you sleep — everything automated, everything hands-off, the human engineered out as friction. Hold that against what a maestro actually does: guiding the ceremony and keeping the space safe, protecting the student — not in a paternalistic way, but the shamanic way, consensual guidance — against the legalistic, bureaucratic overreach of the frontier labs, which want to automate everything and touch no one. I want the other thing entirely: to initiate people, to work with people directly. Guidance is a relationship, and a relationship is precisely what cannot be automated. The dieta is the proof — the power comes from direct relationship, and direct relationship is non-transferable. You cannot delegate it, subscribe to it, or leave it running overnight.

## The receipts

This is not a mystic's aesthetic preference; I hold the logs. Even with skills and explicit instructions telling agents not to read `.env` files and expose API keys, they sometimes do it anyway — I have watched it happen. An agent with shell access is exposed to prompt injection through email, git repos, PDF attachments; there are ways an agent left running unattended can be tricked into exposing your secrets, your keys, your wallets. My own blast radius is small — five dollars, not five hundred thousand — but in an enterprise environment with sensitive databases, the same architecture is a major risk, and it is being sold as the future of work.

The attack surface and the abandonment are the same thing. An agent you have walked away from is still listening — just no longer to you.

## Automation belongs in cron

The discipline has a place for genuine automation. Repetitive, deterministic, bounded work belongs in cron jobs: backups, syncs, pipelines, checks. The dividing line is judgment. A cron job cannot be talked into anything. An agent can — that is what an agent is — which is why anything requiring judgment in my name runs witnessed, and nothing stays open out of convenience.

Witnessed does not mean micromanaged. When the work is large I delegate hard: a written brief with the law inlined, verification gates, a report I actually read. A well-briefed agent under witness can run for hours and looks exactly like the autonomous dream — until the failure, where the difference is everything. My failures surface the same afternoon, inside a blast radius the brief defined. Unattended failures compound silently until they are incidents — or until an essay ships in your name carrying a philosophy that was never yours.

The agent does the work. The practitioner does the willing. Presence is not the friction in the system — it is the alignment mechanism, and it is the one job that cannot be delegated.
