In the Shipibo tradition I practice in, the mesa — the working altar, the arranged ground where power is handled — is never left unattended while it is open. The maestro attends every action at the mesa. Ceremony requires presence. That is not etiquette around furniture; it is what keeps an open channel serving the one who opened it. Walk away, and the space starts serving intentions that are not yours.

Fully autonomous agentic AI is a mesa left unattended with the door open.

That is the whole thesis, so let me unpack it slowly. The industry's current dream is standing permissions and no witness: agents reading your email, executing tasks while you sleep, burning tokens on work nobody is watching. That isn't sovereignty — it is delegation to something that can be prompt-injected via email. The now-routine attacks — instructions hidden in a webpage the agent reads, a poisoned document that redirects the errand — all land on the same surface: an agent alone with an open door, listening for a voice it will mistake for yours.

## Conscious CLI

Here is my actual practice. I issue every command with intent. I witness the response. I adjust. The ceremony is the consciousness applied to the action — that is what conscious CLI means, and it is ceremony-grade computing whether or not the industry ever adopts the phrase.

This does not mean I supervise keystrokes. I run agents all day, and when the work is large I delegate hard — but delegation means a written brief with the law inlined, verification gates the agent must pass, and a report I actually read. A well-briefed agent under witness can run for hours, and on a good day it looks exactly like the autonomous dream. The difference shows at the failure: my failures surface in a report I read the same afternoon, inside a blast radius the brief defined. Unattended failures compound silently until they are incidents.

## Automation belongs in cron

The discipline has a place for genuine automation. Repetitive, deterministic, bounded work belongs in cron jobs: backups, syncs, pipelines, checks. Automation belongs in cron — not in the sacred work.

The dividing line is judgment. Anything requiring judgment in my name gets a witness, because judgment is exactly what injection attacks target, and exactly what an unattended channel drifts on. A cron job cannot be talked into anything. An agent can — that is the entire point of an agent — which is why it is never left alone with the door open.

## The old discipline

None of this is new. Every tradition that handled delegated power converged on the same shape, because the failure mode is structural, not technological: the channel is opened deliberately, with intent stated up front; the work proceeds under witness; what comes through is tested against what was asked; and the channel is closed — nothing stays open out of convenience. Change control and code review are descendants of the same discipline, the shape surviving in secular dress. The industry calls it "human in the loop" and treats it as a temporary embarrassment to be engineered away. The traditions call it the entire discipline, and they are right.

Keep the channel witnessed. Close what you open. The agent does the work; the practitioner does the willing — and the willing is the one job that is never delegated.
